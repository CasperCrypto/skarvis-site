HERMES · drone detection node

Know when a drone is over your site.

HERMES listens for drone radio signals. You get an alert, a direction and an incident record for your protection plan.

Join the pilot list Build status
Remote ID
Mandatory in the EU since 1 January 2024 for class-marked drones. HERMES reads the serial number and the drone and pilot positions.
Direction finding
Five antennas point to the 433 MHz, 868 MHz and 1.2 GHz links that FPV and long-range drones use.
Receive-only
Receive-only devices need no radio permit in Poland (art. 145 of the Electronic Communications Law).
Polish incident report
Each alarm becomes a record for the site's protection file.

A problem that just became an obligation.

Since the 29 May 2026 amendment to Poland's crisis management act, critical infrastructure operators must include the drone threat in their threat analysis and site protection plan.

Most systems on the market were designed for the military, with neutralisation features a civilian site cannot legally use. What is missing is a layer that detects and documents.

From signal to report.

  1. Listen

    HERMES goes on a mast or roof. It receives Remote ID over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth and finds the direction of drone control links. No cameras, no emissions of any kind.

  2. Alert

    When a drone enters the zone, the person on duty gets an alert with its confidence level. Confidence is shown, not hidden.

  3. Prove

    Every alarm keeps its raw evidence. From it comes a Polish incident report: time, track, serial number, direction and pilot position when the drone broadcasts it.

Anything that doesn't broadcast Remote ID, including most hostile drones.

Direction finder

Hears
Control and video links at 433 MHz, 868 MHz and 1.2 GHz, used by FPV and long-range drones. Regardless of whether the drone is registered or broadcasts Remote ID at all — including home-built craft.
Gives
A bearing from one node, a position from two.
Misses
2.4 and 5.8 GHz links, and drones with no radio link at all.

One event, one format.

Every sensor writes events in the same JSON format, one per line. The software runs today on simulated signals and passes 165 automated tests.

  • Zones and a list of authorised flights, such as your own inspection drones
  • Operator acknowledgement and a persistent event log
  • Polish incident report, PDF or HTML, for the site's protection file
  • Detection and records with no internet; the internet only adds remote access

What HERMES doesn't do, and what it can't see.

Honest limits, before you ask.

Doesn't do

  • It doesn't jam or take over a drone. The device only receives. It has no transmit path.
  • It doesn't neutralise. A person decides on any action against a drone, under the law, including art. 156ze of Poland's Aviation Law.
  • It doesn't replace radar. It is a low-cost detection layer that is quick to deploy, not a military system.

Can't see

  • Fibre-optic drones and drones with no radio link. Those need radar or cameras. Fusion with such sensors is planned for version 2.
  • 2.4 and 5.8 GHz drones that don't broadcast Remote ID. The direction finder only hears bands below 1766 MHz.
  • Coordinates from a single node. A bearing is a direction. A position needs two nodes.
  • A reliable baseline from day one. In signal-dense surroundings false hits rise, so the system learns the background during a baseline period.
